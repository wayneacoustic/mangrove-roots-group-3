01 · ABOUT SIPAWAY'S MANGROVES

What Are Mangroves?

Mangroves are salt-tolerant trees that grow along coastlines.

In Sipaway Island, the mangrove forest serves as an important habitat for fish, crabs, shellfish, birds, and other wildlife.


The mangrove forest is located in Sipaway Island, San Carlos City, Negros Occidental.


Aside from mangroves, Sipaway Island is also rich in coral reefs, marine resources, agricultural land, coconut plantations, and clean coastal waters.


These natural resources support fishing, agriculture, ecotourism, and the livelihood of the local community.

02 · WHY DO MANGROVES MATTER?

Why should we protect them?

Mangroves provide important benefits to Sipaway Island and its community.

🛡️

Coastal Protection

Mangroves act as a natural shield that helps protect coastlines from strong waves, storm surges, and coastal erosion.

🐟

Marine Habitat

Mangroves provide shelter and habitat for fish, crabs, birds, and other wildlife.

🤝

Community Livelihood

Mangroves support marine life that contributes to food security and the daily income of local fishermen.

☁️

Climate Benefits

Mangroves help fight climate change by absorbing carbon from the air.

03 · THE PROBLEM

What Threatens Sipaway's Mangroves?

Although the mangrove forest is generally healthy in many areas, it continues to face threats caused by human activities.

Major Threats

  • Illegal cutting of mangrove trees
  • Improper waste disposal
  • Pollution
  • Oil or hazardous-material spills
  • Destructive fishing practices

The problem affects more than trees.

These activities can damage mangrove forests, coral reefs, and other marine ecosystems.


They can threaten marine habitats, contribute to declining fish populations, reduce biodiversity, and affect the livelihood of local fishermen.


Polluted waters and damaged coastal areas may also make the island less attractive to tourists.

04 · CONSERVATION

Community Conservation Efforts

A Healthy Forest

The mangrove forest in Sipaway Island is healthy in many areas and is generally well-maintained because of community conservation efforts.


Conservation initiatives are led by the Sipaway Eco-tourism Workers Association (SETWA) under the municipal Biodiversity and Landscape Approach in Nature Conservation (BALANCE) Program.

Conservation Partnerships

These efforts align with the Negros Island Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan (NIBSAP) 2018–2028.
